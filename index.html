02Delta × SCAD Pro · 2026

The future of travel planning

Create a personalized travel experience that inspires Gen Z and builds long-term loyalty to Delta.

Scenario

Gen Z isn’t very involved with the Delta brand, and people only open the app to check in and get a boarding pass. Inspiration happens off-site, and delta.com mostly shows prices and one picture. Delta wants to build Gen Z engagement today to drive loyalty tomorrow.

The concept: WanderList

Their line was “turn bucket lists into boarding passes.” It’s a feature inside the Fly Delta app (iOS and Android) that gives Gen Z destination inspiration, lets them see themselves on the trip, and creates shareable, personalized moments.

What did I do? Project Manager

As Project Manager, I contributed to competitive analysis, took part in research through interviews, surveys and focus groups, tested and ideated the SkyMiles prototype, and developed a service blueprint to visualize how the whole experience connects.

Secondary research

28%projected growth of the global super-app market

“It will surpass $400bn market size by 2030.”

Kearney, From App to Ecosystem: How to Scale into a Mobility Super App. Accessed 30 June 2026.

Competitor analysis

Leading the opportunity gap

Six airlines scored across seven categories: price, UI/UX, social media, transparency, personalization, seamless experience and inspiration for trips. Every line collapses on the last one.

Opportunity space

The opportunity isn’t to redesign booking. It’s to redesign the relationship between Gen Z and travel before booking ever begins.

None of the main competitors have started to add mechanics to trip inspiration, a subject people handle directly on their social media.

How might we break the bridge of Instagram and directly provide the inspiration?

Primary research · interviews and surveys

“I find travel inspiration on social media constantly, but only book when I can financially afford it.”

Social media

Indulgence & cost effectiveness

App opportunities · defining what to design

The MQDs and SkyMiles are confusing.

“Why can’t I buy anything with 15,000 miles?”

Ambiguity on partner involvement.

“Why have I been sent to a 7th site?”

Features like the medallions and miles are complex to find.

“Why did I have to go through 20 things just to check my features?”

My design contribution · after 85 user tests

  • Easy to use. People liked seeing a map that shows how far their rewards can take them.
  • Confusing numbers. Users struggled to understand how rewards convert to flight prices.
  • Clarify language. Avoid the term “miles” if it causes confusion.
  • Show price upfront. Many users missed the price completely.

Delta Beyond · blueprinting the full solution

I created the back-stage and front-stage flow to make the whole experience happen.

Project scope statement

A service blueprint documenting the “Delta Beyond” experience across the Fly Delta app — mapping the full system of interactions, operations and infrastructure from Discover, through creating a trip, to Remember.

Sample

Based on 75 interviewees, 140 survey results (United States), an internal workshop and secondary research.

Context

Built by mapping the customer journey across stages, then layering in the touch-points, front-stage responses, back-stage operations and support infrastructure that enable each moment.

The horizontal axis is time, the customer’s progression in the system. The vertical axis is visibility: from what the customer directly experiences at the top, to the invisible systems and processes that power the service at the bottom.

03Movistar · Intern Service Designer · 2024

Movistar Game Club

Scenario

Movistar Game Club is part of a pioneering esports and video game entertainment producer in Colombia. They make experiences for every kind of gamer, from casual players to professionals and the people who watch, by running gaming center activations, building teams, and connecting content creators with academies, tournaments, and regular rental hours.

Problem

People don’t attend the center because of the high prices, and prefer to stay home. The venue sells hours of equipment, but the community doesn’t resonate with that business model.

Why was I hired?

To analyze the first 3 months of operations and to research (interviews, surveys, maps), assisting and prototyping with the goal of bringing in more people.

My contribution

I researched and revealed how people visit the venue for the community, the atmosphere and the feeling of belonging. I designed events around what happens beyond the competition: where spectators connect, communities come together, and shared experiences create belonging. I also worked on the backstage, to see how efficiently we can create an event.

Research · regular events

Competitions focused on prizes for the winner.

Key finding: the community wasn’t looking for a competitive landscape. Regular tournaments are dominated by the same players, who live off them. The different levels make the tournaments frustrating, and regular players stop going after 2 events.

For the initial events, the purpose was to standardize event organization with clear tasks along the way. I was expected to highlight friction points during organization and realization.

Persona · “Esports team fan”

Juan, 16

LoL fan · Aspiring competitor · Introverted · Forum participant

Juan lives in northern Bogotá with his parents. In his free time he watches Twitch and plays LoL with friends online, whom he met through streamer fandoms and forums on Discord or Reddit. He especially loves competition and never misses one for the team.

Key needs

Tension grows at home during events because of noise and crowds late at night. A place with in-person watch parties would make Juan a very likely user.

Interview takeaways

People refused to go to the venue by themselves for competitions.

“the gang gets together”

The moment when the community meets people they already know creates a sense of belonging — the highest-leverage moment found in our interview process.

Players come for a pro experience, not just competition. Pro-level conditions, expert casting and a cheering crowd turn a tournament into a moment amateur players remember — and that feeling makes them want to improve and come back.

How might we make every amateur player feel like they’re on a pro stage, from the setup to the casting to the crowd?

Synthesis of research

A stakeholder map, used to see every task and its actor. It allowed us to see the full flow between different teams, especially teams that don’t know each other.

Current system

The community manager (CM) was connected to almost everyone, so messages to brands, influencers and activations competed with logistics. The CM couldn’t multitask, which made the rest of the team rush.

Proposed system

I redesigned the CM flow: rebalanced the CM’s tasks, distributed the rest, focused the CM on brands, and integrated communication so every member is aware of each task.

Events focus on viewers · changing the focus of the competition

Proposed system

Developed two events focused on the viewer, not the player: Fortnite × Metallica and the Leviathan watch party (exclusivity). We built a full area of activities and displays, and tested a new task plan for creating these events.

500+attendees and 1M+ impressions

130+hours rented during events

04Bliss · UX class project · Data analyst · 2025

Wellness in students’ lives

Students’ packed schedules create barriers to maintaining overall well-being.

Problem

As they struggle to balance multiple aspects of wellness, their mental, physical and spiritual health often suffer under the weight of academic pressures. They need clear, accessible, community-based solutions designed to fit into busy lives.

What is Bliss?

A community-driven yoga app designed to help students integrate wellness into their busy lives. It provides accessible tools for stress management, physical activity and mindfulness, all within a supportive community: a class calendar, personalized profiles, an AI personal trainer and a community page.

My role · data analyst

I worked in the research stage, within primary research — fly on the wall, 45 surveys and 8 interviews — looking for the scope of the project: how to seamlessly integrate wellness into students’ lives.

  • Participated in the creation of the rainbow sheet
  • Participated in the development of personas
  • Finding opportunities and gaps
  • Learning UX essentials

73.8%of students spend 1–3 hours a week on self-care of any kind

52.4%don’t do yoga because of lack of time

60%are interested in joining a yoga club

Understanding the journey · a day in the life of a senior

  1. 6:00 amMorning run and breakfast
  2. 8:00 amStudio classes
  3. 1:30 pmLunch
  4. 2:00 pmWorks on assignments
  5. 6:00 pmDinner and social break
  6. 7:30 pmStudio work / studying
  7. 10:30 pmChecks social media before bed

Rainbow sheet

42 answers, one grid

Every participant coded across age, year, stressors, coping habits, time of day and interests — the pattern finder behind the insights.

What we discovered

  • Students value a non-judgmental, beginner-friendly atmosphere that makes wellness practices more approachable and inclusive.
  • Smaller class sizes and access to at-home practice resources help students stay engaged and build consistent wellness habits.
  • A flexible scheduling system with multiple time slots allows students to fit wellness activities into their busy routines.
  • Virtual class options provide an accessible alternative for those with scheduling conflicts, ensuring more students can participate.

Synthesis

Bliss strives to cultivate…

  • an open and welcoming space where students can seamlessly incorporate yoga into their daily routines
  • an empowering platform that helps students embrace a peaceful lifestyle
  • a holistic approach to wellness that helps students prioritize self-care

Ideation · four how-might-we

  1. How might we provide students with effective, long-term stress management solutions?
  2. How might we foster a sense of community through wellness activities?
  3. How might we create a more inclusive and flexible approach to wellness that fits students’ busy schedules?
  4. How might we design a space that fosters relaxation and encourages participation?

Five whys · community

  1. College students struggle to connect in yoga settings.
  2. Students have a fear of judgment.
  3. Students feel uncomfortable with new people.
  4. No friends to introduce you.
  5. You don’t have friends at the yoga club.

Root problemYou’re meeting these people for the first time in person.

UX opportunityCreate a personalized profile and an interactive community page.

The concept

A wellness app where students can seamlessly track their health, book yoga classes, vibe with friends and empower others to live out a peaceful lifestyle.

Personal journal

A personalized habit tracker for students who struggle to find time for wellness. Build mindful routines one step at a time.

Yoga AI trainer

Through a guided questionnaire, this smart companion understands your needs and recommends the perfect yoga flow.

Community hub

Discover posts from fellow members, share your journey, and dive into discussions tailored to your favorite yoga styles.
